MyDeloadTracker is an AI strength coach for serious lifters. It answers the
one question workout apps never answer: should I push today, or back off?
It reads your logged sets and your recovery data, scores your readiness out
of 100, and tells you when to deload.

Design it like a precision instrument, not a lifestyle app. Dark by
default, near-black cool ink rather than pure black, confident typography,
generous space, calm. Numbers are the hero: big, tabular, immediately
readable at arm's length in a gym. This gets filmed on a phone for
investors, so every screen must look composed in a vertical 393x852 frame.
Avoid neon green on black, avoid cream and terracotta wellness palettes.
One restrained accent colour for identity, plus three semantic states that
must stay clearly distinguishable: good, caution, and back off.

Design these nine screens:

1. Login. Email, password, sign in, a sign up link, and the brand mark.
   Quiet and fast.

2. Onboarding. Bodyweight, sex, and units, then pick your main lifts and
   enter a recent working set for each. Should feel like calibrating an
   instrument, about sixty seconds of work.

3. Home. The hero is today's readiness: a large score out of 100, a small
   waveform sparkline of the last eight weeks, a one-line verdict such as
   "Push today" or "Back off", and one sentence of reasoning. Below it, a
   compact activity strip of the last two weeks of training days, then four
   large tappable tiles that open Log, Progress, History, and the AI coach.
   This screen must fit one phone screen with no scrolling.

4. Insights. The readiness breakdown: each fatigue factor as a labelled row
   with its contribution, a deload alert card listing the specific reasons
   it fired, and the plan for the coming week.

5. Log. A search field pinned at the top with instant typeahead results
   showing exercise name, muscle group, and equipment. Below it the current
   workout: each exercise as a card with a grid of sets, each set a row of
   reps, weight, and an optional effort rating. A rest timer and a daily
   check-in card at the bottom. Every weight input needs room for a short
   sentence above it explaining what to enter, for example the weight of a
   single dumbbell, or the total on the bar.

6. Scan, the camera flow, in three states. First, a live camera view with a
   framing hint and a record button. Second, a processing state that names
   what is happening in stages: reading the plates, identifying the lift,
   counting the reps. Third, and most important, the result card: the lift
   name, then the weight and the rep count as two very large readouts side
   by side, each tappable to correct, one primary button to log the set.
   Some fields may be uncertain and need to be visibly flagged for review
   without looking like an error.

7. Progress. A strength standards card that bands each lift from Beginner
   to Elite with a progress bar toward the next band, a lift lookup with a
   line chart of estimated one rep max over time, and personal records.

8. History. Past sessions as cards, each showing the date, the exercises,
   and the sets, scannable at a glance.

9. Coach. A chat with the AI coach: the athlete's messages and the coach's
   longer formatted answers with headings and bullet points.

Use a bottom tab bar with exactly four tabs: Home, Log, Progress, and
Coach. Settings lives behind a gear icon in the top bar, not in the tab
bar. Tap targets at least 44 pixels. Copy should be calm and short, with no
exclamation points.
